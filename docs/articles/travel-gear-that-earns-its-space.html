The best travel gear isn't the flashiest or the most-reviewed — it's the stuff you reach for on every trip because it solves a real problem without adding bulk. Here's what actually matters when you're choosing each of these five essentials.

Packing Cubes Set

Look for a set with at least three graduated sizes, a breathable mesh top, and a two-way zipper that runs the full length so you can open the cube flat. Compression or "expandable" zippers matter less than durable seams and self-healing zippers, since a blown seam mid-trip is the main way these fail. Skip sets with rigid frames or hard plastic bases — they eat space and force awkward shapes into your bag.

Portable Luggage Scale

The feature that matters most is a display you can read while the bag is still hanging, so a backlit screen and a peak-hold (or "lock") function beat raw accuracy numbers — most cheap units are accurate to within a pound anyway. Choose a unit with a sturdy steel hook and a comfortable strap handle, and check the maximum capacity is at least 40 kg if you ever check heavy bags. Lithium coin-cell power beats batteries that can die in your drawer between trips.

Universal Travel Adapter

Confirm it actually covers the region you're visiting and handles your device voltage, then prioritize grounding and built-in surge or overload protection over a low price. The genuinely useful differentiator is how many simultaneous devices it can charge — look for one with multiple USB-C ports (ideally with PD fast-charging output) plus at least one grounded AC socket, so one adapter covers your phone, laptop, and camera at once. A compact shape that doesn't block adjacent outlets or fall out of loose sockets is worth more than a dozen country labels.

Compression Toiletry Bag

What you're really buying is the right balance of organization and packability, so look for a wide, flat opening that lets you see everything at once instead of a narrow mouth you have to dig through. Waterproof or leak-proof lining, plus a removable clear liquids pouch for airport security, is what separates a good one from a leaky roll-up. External compression straps or a slim profile matter more than sheer capacity, since a toiletries kit that bulges defeats the purpose of "compression."

Neck Pillow With Storage

First make sure it's actually comfortable — a memory-foam fill that supports your neck without pushing your head forward, and an adjustable clasp so it sits correctly — because no amount of storage rescues a pillow you won't wear. Then check that the pocket is secure (a zippered, hidden compartment beats an open pouch) and large enough for the one or two items you actually need in-flight, like a passport, phone, and earplugs. Washable, removable covers are the quiet feature that keeps a travel pillow usable long-term.
