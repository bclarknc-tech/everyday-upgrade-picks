Upgrading a home office rarely means buying more stuff — it means fixing the small physical pain points that make long hours uncomfortable. Here's what actually matters when choosing five of the most common upgrades.

Monitor Arm Mount

The two things that matter most are weight capacity and VESA compatibility. Check your monitor's weight and its VESA bolt pattern (usually 75x75 or 100x100 mm), and make sure the arm is rated for both before buying. Look for a mount that holds its position without sagging and has cable channels built in, since those are the difference between a clean setup and a messy one. Clamp mounts are generally more stable than grommet mounts but require a desk edge that isn't hollow.

Ergonomic Keyboard

The key features are split, tenting, and the ability to reduce wrist extension, not just a fancy layout. A slight negative tilt or tenting angle can reduce forearm strain far more than a cushioned wrist rest, so check whether the keyboard actually lets you adjust its angle. Mechanical switches matter less than most reviews suggest — what counts is that the key spacing, travel, and layout let you keep your wrists straight while typing.

Under-Desk Footrest

The critical spec is adjustability, since the right height depends on your chair and desk, not a fixed setting. A rockable or tilting surface is genuinely useful because it lets you shift your legs through the day instead of locking them in one position. Surface texture matters too — you want enough grip that your feet stay put, but not so much that it catches your socks or scratches shoes.

Cable Management Box

Capacity and heat are the specs that actually decide whether one works, since power strips and power bricks generate real warmth when enclosed. Look for one large enough to hold your surge protector plus its adapters with air left over, and prefer vented or breathable designs over fully sealed boxes. A lid that lifts off cleanly rather than a hinged flap will save you frustration every time you need to unplug one thing.

Adjustable Laptop Stand

Height range and stability are what you're really paying for — the goal is raising the screen to eye level, so measure how high you actually need it before buying. A stand that wobbles when you type defeats its purpose, so look for wide feet, a solid build, and a surface that grips the laptop rather than letting it slide. Ventilation matters too, since raising a laptop can trap heat against a solid metal plate if the stand has no gaps or airflow channels.
